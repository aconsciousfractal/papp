# Documentary research

Use this module when answering a question through documents, editions,
archives or attributed statements. Begin with the question and the exact
claim the material could support. Reuse a sufficient bibliography, source
note or project account. Add a record only when the answer needs a locator,
qualification or unresolved issue that is not already preserved there.

Separate three judgments: what the supplied copy says; whether its edition,
authorship or attribution is authenticated; and whether the assertion itself
is true. A readable passage can support a report about that copy even when
its origin is uncertain. An authenticated publication establishes its
documentary identity, not every assertion it contains. Label transcription,
translation, paraphrase and interpretation when that distinction affects the
answer. Keep enough surrounding context to avoid changing the passage's
meaning.

Give a practical locator for evidence actually used: author or attributed
author, title, edition or version where relevant, date, page or section, and
an accessible identifier or artifact reference. Distinguish a printed page
number from a scan index when they differ. Do not invent missing metadata;
identify the gap and its consequence. Recipient access and permission to
redistribute are separate questions.

When sources conflict, expose the disagreement that matters to the question.
Check whether editions, definitions, dates or reporting perspectives explain
it before choosing an interpretation. Qualify chronology by the evidence:
composition, publication and later recollection are different events. An
earliest located occurrence is bounded by the sources searched. Absence in a
defined collection does not establish universal absence. Preserve unanswered
questions rather than resolving them through narrative smoothness.

Historical and withdrawn material may document what was proposed, circulated
or later rejected. Describe that status and period. Its preservation does not
restore scientific credit or make a withdrawn assertion an accepted premise.

**Example.** A supplied scan contains a dated attribution, while a later
edition names another author. Cite both passages and edition details, report
the difference, and leave authorship unresolved unless supporting evidence
settles it.

**When this is unnecessary.** Copying a short passage for the user's own
reading may need only its existing locator and an accurate transcription;
it does not require a new bibliography or authentication project.

**Dependent-use limit.** If a conclusion requires authenticated authorship,
an uncertain copy cannot supply it. Defer that conclusion while continuing
the passage analysis and other work whose support is sufficient.
