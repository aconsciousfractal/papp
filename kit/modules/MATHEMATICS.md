# Mathematics: preserve what the result actually says

Use this when the task makes or consumes mathematical statements, proofs,
computations or certificates. Start with the exact question and the kind of
answer needed. An explanation, finite search and general theorem have different
completion criteria; let the work determine the necessary evidence.

Reuse a sufficient notebook, proof note, source account or governed ledger.
State definitions, domain, hypotheses and excluded cases beside the conclusion.
Identify the proposition and supporting passage rather than relying on a title
or a newer summary. If notation or a construction changes, state the mapping,
properties preserved and information lost.

Keep finite replay distinct from general proof. For computation, preserve the
actual code/command, parameters, checked range, selected results and limitations
needed to inspect the finite conclusion. A numerical match is not an exact
certificate. An exact certificate needs the artifact, checker and trusted basis
on which acceptance depends. A formal proof needs the actual proof environment
and checked artifact; generated prose supplies no such evidence.

For theorem import, identify the exact source proposition and hypotheses and
show which hypotheses apply to the target. Preserve any unresolved obligation.
Keep a proof's availability, mathematical support and review status separate.
A public preprint may be unreviewed, and a reviewed proof may remain private.
State the reviews actually performed and any review required by the consuming
project. Review does not itself authorise publication. State relevant
contributors and exposure; another agent's agreement does not establish
independent review.

Example: an enumeration finds a property for all supplied instances through
n = 12. Report that finite coverage and the method. A conjecture for all n
remains a conjecture unless a general argument supplies the missing step.
The finite search may still be a useful completed task.

When a route fails, retain the obstruction or checked exclusion, useful surviving
lemmas and actual reopening condition. A cancelled search is not a refutation.
An atlas may link these routes but cannot grant proof credit.

For a basic explanation with no new or imported claim, cite the relevant known
fact and its conditions inline; no new computation log or proof ledger is
needed. Existing governed records still remain required where applicable.
If a decisive hypothesis, certificate or premise is unavailable, defer only
the conclusion using it and continue independent authorised work.
