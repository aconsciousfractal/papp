# Optional atlas: a map to the work

Use an atlas when people or agents repeatedly need to locate sources, compare
routes, understand dependencies or preserve what survived a failed attempt.
Start with a README section or existing index. A separate application is optional.
This guide contains manual guidance, no executable rich generator.

## Open, find, inspect

Open the project question, a concrete example if its IDs do not orient the
reader, a qualified checkpoint, the main limit and next action/owner. Find a
selected object by useful title and its native identifier where available.
Inspect its exact qualified original rather than accepting the card alone.

A locator declares its base, complete literal path and separate section/object
selector. Check the reached object as well as file existence. Encode reserved
characters in clickable paths; keep the literal path accessible. A portable
navigation link and delivery of its source payload are different promises.

## Add the view that answers a question

Bibliography can show source-to-result uses. A route view can compare requirements,
transformations, losses and next steps. A graph can show typed relationships,
including excluded dependencies under filters; chronology is not implication.
Retained negative attempts show what was excluded, what remains useful and
what change would justify reopening. Activate only views with a useful purpose
and a responsible curator. Illustrations/themes may change presentation while
preserving readable text and source qualifications.

Declare the indexed set and omissions. Absence from a selected index does not
prove absence from the corpus. Keep hypotheses, domain, limits and unresolved
conflicts accessible even when long dossiers are hidden. A successor should
show the known disposition of an older conclusion pertinent to the use.

## Keep the promised surface current

Reconcile meaning when the source, interpretation or owner checkpoint changes.
A review belongs to the interpreted account, evidence use and checkpoint it
examined, not just unchanged source bytes. An export identifies that qualified
snapshot and what currentness was checked; otherwise label it historical or
unverified. Do not silently carry old review credit forward.

Example: two research routes share an open premise. A filtered graph may hide
the premise but must still expose the dependency and its qualification.

If the existing entry already lets the reader open, find and inspect the work,
no extra atlas file is needed. If maintenance is excessive, narrow its owned
surface or use locators only. Missing reconciliation defers the dependent
current use; it does not require stopping unrelated work or scanning all sources.
